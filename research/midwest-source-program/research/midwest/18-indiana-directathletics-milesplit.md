# 18. Indiana DirectAthletics / MileSplit

Status: complete
Observed on: 2026-09-19

Headline: DirectAthletics' Indiana high-school surface is **two hosts, not one**. The state subdomain
**`https://indiana.tfrrs.org`** ("TFRRS | IHSAA Track & Field Rankings and Meet Results", © 2026
DirectAthletics, Inc.) gives the roster layer: one request enumerates all 901 Indiana HS gender-teams
and one request per team returns **names + numeric AthleteID + season-scoped grade**, the cheapest
Class-of-2027 discovery path found in this assignment — selected with `&year=JR`, **not** `&year=SR`,
because these are 2025-26 season lists (see the class-attribution rule under Athlete evidence). The **parent domain**
`www.directathletics.com` adds what the subdomain lacks: `/leagues/track/123.html` → **929 numeric
TeamIDs** in one request, a meet index carrying the full Indiana HS indoor season (26 HSR-marked rows
on page 1 of the 2025-08…2026-09 window), and year-less performance lists such as
`/lists/track/1428_5490.html?limit=1000` → **3,978 athletes with grade, team, mark, meet and date in one
request**, using the same AthleteID namespace as the subdomain.
The weakness of the TFRRS *subdomain search* is 2026 meet coverage — 56 track meets and **1** XC meet
for 2026, versus 1,022 track and 128 XC for 2025 — but the parent-domain index shows that gap is a
subdomain-search artefact, not a platform gap. MileSplit Indiana complements both on outdoor
meet-calendar breadth (≥100 unique 2026 HS outdoor meets; ≥50 2026 HS XC meets on page 1 alone) and
exposes a literal **"Class of YYYY"** on public athlete pages, but gates performances behind a
subscription and puts its enumerable data layer behind `robots.txt`-disallowed `/api/`.
Neither source emits a single Athletic.net URL or ID in any of the 58 response bodies captured from
them.

### Enumeration

**A. Indiana school/team universe (DirectAthletics/TFRRS) — 1 request for the whole state.**
`GET https://indiana.tfrrs.org/leagues/123.html` returns the "Indiana" league page with two side-by-side
columns, MEN'S TEAMS and WOMEN'S TEAMS, each row a link. Parsed result:
**452 unique team slugs**, **452 boys links** (`/teams/tf/<Slug>_m.html`) and **449 girls links**
(`/teams/tf/<Slug>_f.html`) = 901 gender-team pages. Three slugs are boys-only in the index
(`Angola`, `McCutcheon`, `New_Haven`) — note `McCutcheon_m` and `New_Haven_m` both returned HTTP 200
team pages, so the missing `_f` entries are a coverage gap, not a fetch failure.
Regular expression used: `href="https://indiana\.tfrrs\.org/teams/tf/([^"]+)_(m|f)\.html">([^<]+)</a>`.

**B. Roster + grade per team (DirectAthletics/TFRRS) — 1 request per team page.**

`<a href="/athletes/<AthleteID>/<Team>/<Name>.html">Last, First</a>` in one cell and
`<td>SR|JR|SO|FR</td>` in the next. Verified on `Franklin_Central_m` → 65 rows
`{FR:5, SO:21, JR:20, SR:19}`. The same page carries sibling links
`/teams/xc/<Slug>_m.html`, `/teams/xc/<Slug>_f.html`, `/teams/tf/<Slug>_f.html` and the team's
IHSAA assignments as league links (`IHSAA Sectional 23` → `/leagues/1652.html`,
`IHSAA Regional 6` → `/leagues/1667.html`, `IHSAA Section 17` → `/leagues/xc/1101.html`,

Caveat measured on 8 sampled boys TF pages: 5 populated (rows 32, 65, 82, 33, 170), 3 empty

The parent domain is not just a college portal, and an earlier revision of this section said the
opposite. Three verified surfaces:

*D1. Per-state team index with numeric TeamIDs.*
`GET https://www.directathletics.com/leagues/track/123.html` (title `DirectAthletics`, 145,059 B,
`text/html; charset=ISO-8859-1`) renders an `Indiana | Sport: Track & Field` page with side-by-side

*D3. Indiana HS performance lists in a second, year-less URL shape.*
`directathletics.com/rankings.html` indexes **two** list shapes, not one: `2,848` TFRRS-shaped links
(`/lists/<ListID>/<Slug>/<Year>/<i|o>`, subdomain hosts) **and 274** DA-shaped year-less links
(`/lists/track/<LeagueID>_<ListID>.html`, parent host). Indiana's Hoosier State Relays lists appear
in the year-less shape, keyed `1428` (HSR Large) and `1429` (HSR Small), list ids `545`/`546` (2010)
through `5490`/`5491` (2026), i.e. 32 lists spanning 2010–2026 with **2021 absent**.
**The yield is limit-dependent, and the page's own default is the small view — this is the single
most important operational fact on the surface.** The list page carries real filter selects:
`limit` (`Top 5/10/20/25/30/**50 selected**/100/200/500`), `event_type` (`All Events` + per-event ids
`46` 60m, `61` 3200m, `50` 60H, `71` 4x200, `73` 4x400, `74` 4x800 …), `gender` (`m`/`f`), and
`year` (`SR`/`JR`/`SO`/`FR`/`8`/`7`/`6`). A bare fetch therefore returns the **Top 50 per event**
view and dramatically understates every count. Measured on `1428_5490` alone:

| view | `<tr` | distinct athletes | bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| bare (default `Top 50`) | 1,176 | **686** | 1,210,584 |
| `?limit=500` | 4,942 | 3,674 | 5,152,534 |
| `?limit=1000` | 5,317 | **3,978** | 5,521,584 |
| `?limit=5000` | 5,317 | 3,978 | 5,521,584 |
| `?limit=1000&year=SR` | 1,166 | **1,000** | 1,243,423 |
| `?limit=2000&year=SR` | 1,166 | 1,000 | 1,243,423 |
| `?limit=5000&year=SR` | 1,166 | 1,000 | 1,243,423 |
| bare `?year=SR` | 323 | 279 | 355,718 |
| `?limit=1000&gender=m` | 2,858 | 2,184 | 2,973,384 |
| `?limit=1000&year=SR&gender=f` | 471 | 393 | 505,708 |

*(The `<tr` column is the raw tag count, which includes one unclosed row, the per-event header rows and a
trailing footer table — see row composition below for how many of those are data.)*

Three rules follow. (a) **Always publish the limit** — the same URL is a 686-athlete page or a
3,978-athlete page depending on one parameter. (b) The unfiltered view **saturates at `?limit=1000`**
(`limit=2000` and `5000` return the same 5,521,584 B and an **identical row multiset**), so there is
no benefit above 1,000 and the
whole Large list costs one request. (c) **Nothing is hidden behind a ceiling — proven, not assumed.**
The `year=SR` view returns exactly 1,000 distinct athletes and the same row multiset at `limit` 1000/2000/
5000, which *looks* like a 1,000-cap; it is not. Splitting that view by gender gives **f 393 + m 607 =
1,000 exactly**, i.e. the parts sum to the whole, so 1,000 is a **real cohort** and the identical
equal sizes mean the **data is exhausted**, not that a cap binds. The same test on `year=JR` gives
**1,155 = f 490 + m 665** exactly — and 1,155 > 1,000 proves there is **no blanket 1,000-athlete cap**
on this route at all. The cheap, general detector is therefore the **gender-split sum check**: fetch
the cohort, fetch `&gender=m` and `&gender=f`, and if the parts exceed the whole the view is truncated.
Run it before trusting any cohort count from this route.
Each individual row links its athlete as `https://www.directathletics.com/athletes/track/<AthleteID>.html`.
**Row width is a property of the EVENT, not of the list, and each event section is internally uniform.**
Measured on the `?limit=1000` capture — 4,512 athlete-linked data rows, plus 775 relay-team rows, 7 footer
rows and 22 `<th>` header rows, i.e. 5,317 `<tr` — the width is `7 + conversion? + wind?`: **7 cells** = `Place | Athlete |
Year | Team | Mark | Meet | Meet Date` (2,510 rows: 60m, 60H, 3200m); **8 cells** = a **conversion
column** inserted before Meet, imperial then metric, e.g. `7' 1.75" | 2.17m` (1,390 rows: HJ, PV, SP);
**9 cells** = conversion **plus a trailing wind cell**, always literally `NWI` in every sampled row — a
column carrying no usable value (612 rows: LJ). **Zero of the 14 athlete-bearing event sections mix
widths**, so the width is fixed per event and can be derived from the event id rather than sniffed per
row. Indiana exhibits only three of the four possible widths because its HSR event set contains no
wind-eligible non-conversion sprint (see the event-set limitation below); a fourth width, wind with no
conversion, is what a state whose meet format includes a 200m produces. Consequently a fixed-index read
of cell 6 as the date is correct on only the 2,510 seven-cell rows and returns the **meet name** on the
other 2,002 — **44.4% of rows**. Locate the date as the last cell matching `^[A-Z][a-z]{2} \d{1,2},
\d{4}$` and take the meet as the cell before it; every one of the 4,512 rows contains exactly one such
date cell, so pattern-based extraction is total and safe. Column roll-up at this limit: **4,501 of 4,512
rows carry a grade** (99.8%), and the grade cells total `SR` 1,144 / `JR` 1,306 / `SO` 1,159 / `FR` 892
= 4,501, i.e. the two independent measurements agree.

**Event-set limitation — the list route covers only 11 events, and this caps athlete yield.** The page's
own `event_type` `<select>` offers exactly: `46` 60 Meters, `50` 60 Hurdles, `61` 3200 Meters, `64` High
Jump, `65` Pole Vault, `66` Long Jump, `68` Shot Put, `71` 4x200, `73` 4x400, `74` 4x800, `78` Distance
Medley Relay — and the rendered sections match that list one-for-one (7 individual + 4 relay, each split
Men/Women). There is **no individual 200m, 400m, 800m, 1600m, 1500m or mile anywhere on the page** — the
string `1600` and `1500` appear zero times, and the only `200`/`400`/`800` occurrences are the relay
labels. This is the Indiana HSR meet format (a 3200m instead of a 1600m, relays instead of open middle
distance), not a truncation. Two consequences for the acquisition flow: an athlete who competes only in
open 200/400/800/1600 does **not** appear on this surface at all, and middle-distance runners surface
only as relay-team rows whose leg names carry no AthleteID (see row composition below), so they are
discovered as names but not as ids. **Row composition, because it bounds the athlete yield:** the default-limit page
holds **1,146 data rows — 746 individual rows carrying an athlete link and 400 relay-team rows** with no
athlete link (e.g. `1 | Franklin
Central | 1:26.56 | Hainje, Keck, Mitchell, Snell | Hoosier State Relays Finals (Large Schools) |
Mar 28, 2026`) — the four leg names exist only as a comma-separated string, so relay legs are **not**
enumerable as athletes from this surface and any athlete who appears only on relays is invisible here.
Of the 746 individual default rows, **735 carry a `Year` grade** and 11 do not.

**Cross-state note (agent-sourced, not reproduced here).** Both figures above are view-dependent and
so is the comparison between states, which means a single cross-state relay fraction would be wrong.
Agent 14 measured the Illinois DA lists at **1,193 relay rows of 11,720 (10.2%) against 3 relay events
of 15**, versus Indiana's **14.7% against 4 of 11** — relay row share tracks relay event share in both,
so the difference is meet format, not extraction. The same source reports a sharper roster difference
that strengthens the claim above: **Illinois team pages have no season scoping at all** (no `select`,
no `seasonSelect`/`config_hnd` reference) and their roster `Year` column is an **era-relative ordinal**
— on Lane Tech roster `13`/`14`/`15` map to the 2024 3A Top Times list's `SO`/`JR`/`SR` for the same
AthleteIDs, so the code rises with seniority without naming the class, and read literally it would
mis-assign a cohort by ~11 years. Consequence for the adapter set, and the reason Indiana's roster
route is usable alone: **a team-page roster yields `Name → AthleteID` in both states, but a
directly usable class token only in Indiana** — season-keyed `FR`/`SO`/`JR`/`SR` under the `config_hnd`
selector — whereas on Illinois the class token must come from the competition lists, whose `Year` is
season-scoped by the list itself. Agent 14's roster column is therefore forbidden input for class
assignment there; Indiana's is usable once the season is resolved.
The 2026 pair is `GET https://www.directathletics.com/lists/track/1429_5491.html?limit=1000` → 200,
3,850,927 B, title `HSR Small School (1A-3A) Performance List` (default-limit 651 athletes from
1,190,812 B). The two lists stay **perfectly disjoint at every limit tested — 0 shared AthleteIDs —
so 2 requests yield a union of 6,533 distinct athletes** (3,978 Large + 2,555 Small), and the same
2 requests with **`&year=JR`** yield a **union of 1,861 Class-of-2027 (1,155 Large + 706 Small,
disjoint)**; `&year=SR` on the same lists yields 1,678, which is the **Class of 2026** — the `Year`
column is the grade *at the meet*, proven below. Measured, not assumed — unlike the IL class lists, where agent 14 found 1 athlete
on two lists with two different marks, Indiana's Large/Small split shows no cross-listing at all.

**The `Year` column is the grade AT THE MEET — proven, and it decides which filter is Class of 2027.**
The 2026 lists are the **2025-26 indoor season** (meet dates `Mar 9-13, 2026`), so a `SR` row there
graduated in spring 2026 and a `JR` row is the Class of 2027. Proof by year-over-year transition:
comparing the 2025 list (`/lists/track/1428_5024.html`, `Mar 8-13, 2025`) with the 2026 list, **1,366
athletes appear in both, and 1,330 of them advanced exactly one grade** (`JR→SR` 507, `SO→JR` 491,
`FR→SO` 332) while only 19 did not move. If the column held each athlete's *current* grade the
transition counts would all be zero-off-diagonal; they are overwhelmingly diagonal, so the column is
season-scoped. **Consequence: on these lists `&year=JR` selects the Class of 2027 and `&year=SR`
selects the Class of 2026.** `JR ∩ SR = 0` on the Large list, exactly as a same-season grade split
requires. (Cross-checked against a peer's independent measurement of `year=JR` = 1,155 on the Large
list, reproduced here exactly.)
**Trap:** the 2026 ids are `1428_5490` (Large) and `1429_5491` (Small) — the list ids are **off by
one across the two leagues**, not shared. Guessing `1429_5490` does **not** 404: it returns HTTP 200
with the *Large* list's own 686 athletes. A collector that merges without checking the rendered title
would see a fake "686/686 overlap" and could quietly double-count the Large cohort. Always confirm
the `title` says `HSR Large School (4A-6A)` vs `HSR Small School (1A-3A)` before merging, and treat a
uniform 100% overlap between two supposed sibling lists as the signature of this fallback rather than
as a data-quality finding.
The athlete ids are the *same namespace* as the TFRRS subdomain's — 4 of the 65 Franklin Central
roster ids appear here with identical names (`8779512` Hainje, `8451185` Keck, `8779511` Mitchell,
`9094932` Lindsey) — so the surfaces join on AthleteID with no name matching.
Year-less lists are used for associations DA serves directly rather than through a TFRRS state
subdomain: `IL Top Times-Class 1A/2A/3A`, `LHSAA Division I/II`, Arizona `AIA`, Delaware, Oregon,
Wyoming and several Florida county conferences. Among the 12 Midwest states, only Indiana has a
TFRRS subdomain (see Enumeration H), so sister assignments must expect the year-less shape elsewhere.

Measured directly: **zero** references to `athletic.net` in any captured response body from either

bundles. Both are direct competitors of Athletic.net (DirectAthletics/TFRRS and FloSports/MileSplit), so:

- Athletic.net meet links: **none exposed**.
- Athletic.net team links: **none exposed**.
- Athletic.net athlete links: **none exposed**.
- Athletic.net result links: **none exposed**.

Seeding is therefore context-only, and the quality of that context differs:

- **TFRRS Indiana seeds deterministically on (name, school, grade, event, meet name, date).** A team
  page yields a `NAME | YEAR` roster table — the name is a single `"Last, First"` cell carrying the
  `AthleteID` link and `YEAR` is one of `SR`/`JR`/`SO`/`FR` — for the entire roster, where the grade is
  scoped to the page's **selected season**, not to today (see the season rule below); an event page
  yields `(place, athlete, YEAR, school, mark, round)`; the meet page yields
  `(meet name, date, venue + city, participating schools)`. That is enough to construct a
  *specific* Athletic.net candidate request (school + name + season) without a broad Athletic.net
  search, and enough to reject a wrong candidate (grade and event must agree).
- **MileSplit Indiana seeds on (name, school, Class of YYYY, city, meet name, date)**, with a numeric
  MileSplit AthleteID for de-duplication inside MileSplit, but the per-athlete event/PR detail that
  would confirm an Athletic.net profile match is subscription-gated.
- No cross-source numeric join exists: TFRRS AthleteID (`8779512`), MileSplit AthleteID
  (`12203866`), and Athletic.net AthleteID (`28872883`, from HAR
  `https://www.athletic.net/athlete/28872883/track-and-field`) are three independent namespaces with
  no published crosswalk. Joins must be name+school+season based.

**Cross-source overlap, measured (method + limits stated).**

*Method A — team universe. Both sides are complete single-page enumerations, so this is the strongest
measurement in this report.* TFRRS side: `GET https://indiana.tfrrs.org/leagues/123.html`, extracted
with `/teams/tf/([^"]+)_(m|f)\.html">([^<]+)</a>` → 452 team slugs. MileSplit side:
`GET https://in.milesplit.com/teams?type=1`, extracted with
`/teams/(\d+)-([^"]+)">([^<]*)</a>` → 533 teams. Normalization: lowercase, strip parentheticals
(`(Gary)`), strip the token set `{high school, hs, community, charter, academy, school, the, inc,
preparatory, prep}`, collapse non-alphanumerics, then apply the observed Fort Wayne alias
`ft → fort wayne` and drop `closed`/`unified` (`FW Bishop Dwenger` ↔ `Ft Wayne Bishop Dwenger`,
`Hammond Clark - Closed` ↔ `Hammond Clark`). Match rule: token-set containment — every token of the
shorter name present in the longer, with one token of slack when the longer name has ≥3 tokens.
**Result: 443 normalized TFRRS names → 438 matched to a MileSplit team (98.9%); 512 normalized
MileSplit names → 491 matched to a TFRRS team (95.9%).** Residuals: TFRRS-only = `campagna`,
`christel house`, `holy cross`, `ihsaa`, `keystone` (5 — note `ihsaa` is a pseudo-team, not a
school). MileSplit-only = 21, dominated by non-IHSAA programs despite the `type=1` HS label:
`crew life track club`, `dave s racing team`, `imboden homeschool`, `indiana athletic association`,
`paramount englewood middle`, `lane middle`, `thomas a edison jr sr`, `matchbook learning at wendell
phillips 63`, `smith for excellence`, `phalen leadership`. **Limits:** name-string matching only, no
ID join; MileSplit's `type=1` filter admits clubs/homeschools/middle schools; TFRRS carries an
`ihsaa` pseudo-team; three slugs are boys-only on TFRRS (`Angola`, `McCutcheon`, `New_Haven`); the
alias set (`ft`) was derived from the observed residuals, so residual counts are a lower bound on
true mismatch.

*Method B — meet universe, 2026 season, Indiana, both providers.* TFRRS side:
`GET /results_search_page.html?search_query=&with_month=&with_sports=track&with_year=2026` and
`&page=2` → 56 meet slugs. MileSplit side:
`GET /results?season=outdoor&level=hs&year=2026` and `&page=2` → 100 unique `MeetID`s. Normalization:
drop a trailing 4-digit year, drop the token set `{invitational, invite, inv, championships,
classic, meet, relays, qualifier, showcase, open, hs}`, collapse non-alphanumerics, intersect.
**Result: 1 shared normalized name (`thursday night spikes`).** The two 2026 calendars are
effectively disjoint: TFRRS's 56 are Hoosier-State-Relays indoor qualifiers plus a handful of spring
duals (`Hoosier_State_Relays_Finals_Large_Schools`, `2026_Indian_Open`, `2026_Warsaw_Tiger_Showcase_1`);
MileSplit's 100 are IHSAA tournament rounds (`IHSAA Boys Sectional 21 at Lawrence Central`,
`IHSAA Regional 1 Boys`, `IHSAA Boys State Track and Field`) plus national/elite meets (AAU Junior
Olympic Games, AAU Region 13, adidas Track Nationals, Brooks PR, Hoka Festival of Miles).
**Limits:** the TFRRS query mixes indoor and outdoor while the MileSplit query is outdoor-only, so
indoor names cannot match; the MileSplit side is a 2-page sample (100 of ≥101) rather than the full
year; and normalization cannot bridge genuinely different naming (e.g. TFRRS
`IHSAA_Sectional_23_Boys` vs MileSplit `IHSAA Boys Sectional 21 At Lawrence Central`, which are
different sectionals anyway). The measurement's force comes from the size of the gap — 56 vs ≥101
meets with 1 shared name — not from the exact match count. *Caveat added after the parent-domain
pass:* the TFRRS-side "56" is the count from the **subdomain search**, which is not the whole 2026

### Incremental use

- **New meets (TFRRS):** `GET https://indiana.tfrrs.org/results.rss` — one request, XML, 30 most
  recent meets with date; and/or
  `GET /results_search_page.html?with_sports={track|xc}&with_year=YYYY&with_month=M` — a
  month-scoped window (30 rows/page), so a weekly collector reads only the current month.
- **New/changed results (TFRRS):** per-team pages expose a `LATEST RESULTS` block with dates — the
  7 sampled boys pages split cleanly into a 2026 indoor cohort (`Zionsville_m`, `McCutcheon_m`,
  `New_Haven_m`, `Andrean_m` all → `March 28, 2026 Hoosier State Relays Finals`) and a 2025 XC
  cohort (`Argos_m` → `October 25, 2025 IHSAA Cross Country Regional 2`, `Attica_m` →
  `October 18, 2025 IHSAA Cross Country Sectional 12`) — plus a per-team season selector
  (`2026 HSR Indoor`, `2025 IHSAA Cross Country`, `2025 Outdoor`, `2024 …`). Re-fetching the ~901
  team pages is the bounded weekly unit; there is no `If-Modified-Since`/digest support and no
  incremental endpoint.
- **New athletes (TFRRS):** re-read the roster table of teams whose `LATEST RESULTS` date advanced;
  rosters are small (32–170 rows) so a diff is cheap.
- **New athletes (MileSplit):** `GET https://in.milesplit.com/sitemap.xml` — one request returns
  7,500 athletes ordered by `<lastmod>` descending, spanning **30 distinct lastmod days
  (`2026-08-21` … `2026-09-19`)** in the captured window, i.e. the whole visible window is roughly one
  month of churn. A weekly collector stores the previous run's `<loc>` set and diffs; the sitemap is
  **not** paginated, so historical backfill is impossible from this source and an athlete not updated
  in the last ~30 days is invisible here.
- **New meets (MileSplit):** `GET /results?season=<s>&level=hs&year=<Y>&page=1` — newest first,
  50 unique meet IDs/page; page 1 is sufficient for a weekly delta.
- **Affected athletes (MileSplit):** a meet's `/meets/<MeetID>-<slug>/results` page loads its athlete
  links client-side from `/api/`, so affected-athlete resolution is *not* available on the
  robots-allowed surface; the sitemap's `<lastmod>` ordering is the usable proxy (an athlete who
  competed gets their page rewritten and floats toward the top of the sitemap).
- No design here requires a full historical re-fetch; the TFRRS 2025/2024 corpus (1,022 + 128 meets)
  can be pulled once and thereafter only delta'd by team page.
- **New meets (parent domain):** `GET /legacy_da/results?filterrific[with_states]=IN&filterrific[with_sports]=track&filterrific[with_date_from]=<last_run>&filterrific[with_date_to]=<today>`
  — a **date-windowed** delta in one request per sport (100 rows/page, `page=N` for more), and it is
  the only surface found that shows Indiana HS duals and middle-school meets alongside HSR qualifiers.
  Because the window is a filter parameter, a weekly collector can ask for exactly the days since its
  last run rather than re-reading a rolling list.
- **HSR performance lists (parent domain):** `/lists/track/1428_<ListID>.html` and
  `1429_<ListID>.html` are **seasonal** — one list id per year (`1428_545` 2010 … `1428_5490` 2026,
  with 2021 absent) — so a collector watches `rankings.html` for a new id in the `1428`/`1429` series
  and re-pulls only on change. During the indoor season the list body is rewritten as qualifiers are
  added, so the bounded weekly delta is a re-pull of the 2 current-season lists (2 requests), not the
  32-list archive.
- **New athletes (parent domain):** there is no athlete feed; new athletes appear as a by-product of
  the HSR lists (D3) and the meet index (D2), both of which carry AthleteIDs in the same namespace as
  the subdomain rosters, so the identity table can be updated by merge rather than by a new sweep.

### Access characteristics

- **No status code is a contract on the list route.** A *well-formed* URL for a nonexistent
  `(league, list)` pair can answer **HTTP 200 with a different list's content** instead of 404ing:
  `GET /lists/track/1429_5490.html` returned the **Large** list's own 686 athletes (title `HSR Large
  School (4A-6A)`, 1,210,584 B) even though the Small sibling is really `1429_5491`. Since that
  response is 200-and-plausible, only the rendered `title` (and a disjointness sanity check) catches
  it. Budget one extra request per list family to confirm the rendered class label.
- **Observed host budget (this assignment, honest count).** Appendix rows document **16 requests to
  `directathletics.com`** (incl. 1 to the apex via redirect), **16 to `indiana.tfrrs.org`** and **1 to
  `www.tfrrs.org`** — ~33 to the DA/TFRRS hosts, plus the two probes recorded in the appendix, i.e.
  **below the ~50/host guidance, sequential, no parallelism, no 429 and no `Retry-After` at any
  point**. A peer independently disclosed ~85-90 requests to `www.directathletics.com` in one run
  (over guidance, no adverse response) — treat that as the observed-tolerance data point, not as
  precedent for a production burst.
- **Do not use byte comparison to test saturation — use multisets.** Two responses of identical size
  from the list route are **not** byte-identical: measured 11,316 differing bytes on an all-grade pair
  and 83-85 on the `year=SR` pair, despite identical row multisets in every case. Two causes: a per-
  request CSRF token, and rows swapping order among equal-mark ties (an equal-time pair at place 127
  can invert between fetches). Measured on my own captures, sequence instability tracks view size: the
  **all-grade** pair (`limit=1000` vs `5000`) differed in row **sequence** while their multisets matched,
  whereas the `year=SR` pairs and a peer's `year=JR` pair were stable in both sequence and multiset —
  so the swap shows up on high-volume views with many equal-mark ties, not universally. So (a) compare
  parsed row **multisets**, never bytes, or a saturation test can false-negative on the token alone;
  and (b) never diff two captures of the same list positionally, since a large view may reorder. Byte comparison *is* valid on genuinely static documents — the MileSplit
  sitemap and its `?page=2` twin are md5-identical — so the rule is route-specific, not universal.
- **DirectAthletics / TFRRS Indiana: normal HTML** (server-rendered Rails, `text/html; charset=utf-8`),
  plus one **static XML** surface (`/results.rss`, `application/xml`) and one **HTML fragment**
  endpoint that behaves like structured data (`/list_data/<ListID>`). No login, no paywall, no
  CAPTCHA, no cookie, no `robots.txt` (HTTP 200 returns the site page, not a directives file, so
  there are no published crawl rules for either `directathletics.com` or `indiana.tfrrs.org`).
  Two parsing caveats on the **parent domain** specifically: (i) `/leagues/{track,xc}/<id>.html` and
  `/lists/track/<l>_<i>.html` are served as **`text/html; charset=ISO-8859-1`**, not UTF-8, so
  scraping them as UTF-8 mangles accented names; (ii) the team-index anchors use **uppercase
  `HREF=`** (`<A class="pLinks" HREF="…/teams/track/56979.html">`), which a lowercase-`href` HTML
  parser or regex silently misses — this is what hid the 929-team index on the first pass.
  One further caveat: `directathletics.com` returns **HTTP 200 for arbitrary unknown paths** (both
  `/robots.txt` and `/sitemap.xml` returned the home page), so status codes on that host cannot be
  trusted — but `/legacy_da/teams` returned a genuine 404 while `/leagues/track/123.html` returned a
  real 145 KB index, so a 404 there is meaningful and a 200 is not automatically meaningful. The
  **league id is only meaningful together with the sport**: `track/123` = Indiana HS T&F (929 team
  ids), `xc/108` = Indiana HS XC (887 team ids), and `xc/123` = a *college* XC league (17 teams) —
  the same integer resolves to two different populations, so ids must be validated by the rendered
  `Sport:` label rather than assumed or carried across sports.
- **MileSplit Indiana: browser application over a public structured-JSON layer, with a
  robots.txt restriction.** `https://in.milesplit.com/robots.txt` (HTTP 200, 173 bytes) publishes:
  `User-agent: Mediapartners-Google / Disallow:` then `User-agent: * / Disallow: /rankings /
  Disallow: /virtual-meets / Disallow: /api/ / Disallow: /contact`, plus `AmazonAdBot: Allow: /`.
  The site's own JS (`https://js.sp.milesplit.com/drivefaze/api.js`) sets `API.endPoint = '/api/'`
  on the state host and `API.apiV3Production = 'https://api.prod.milesplit.com/int/v3/'`.
  `GET https://api.prod.milesplit.com/robots.txt` returns **HTTP 403 `{"message":"Forbidden"}`**.
  **This assignment did not request any `/api/` path and did not call the v3 API** — the
  robots.txt directive was honoured; every MileSplit number above comes from `/teams`, `/results`,
  `/athletes/<id>-<slug>` and `/sitemap.xml`, which the same robots.txt allows. The enumerable
  surface is therefore exactly: 533 teams, 50 meet IDs/page, and the 7,500-athlete sitemap window.
  Athlete-by-event or athlete-by-class enumeration requires `/rankings` (disallowed) or `/api/`
  (disallowed); Main should treat that as a policy decision, not a technical one.
- **Paywall boundary (MileSplit):** athlete profiles are public but performance detail is not —
  the sampled profile renders `To see all performances, subscribe to Join Now`. Class-of-YYYY is on
  the free side of that boundary.
- **Published limits / Retry-After:** none published by either source, and **no 429 or
  `Retry-After` header was observed** in 44 requests to `indiana.tfrrs.org`, 11 to
  `www.directathletics.com`, 14 to `in.milesplit.com`, 4 to `js.sp.milesplit.com`, 1 to
  `api.prod.milesplit.com` or 5 to `www.athletic.net`. All requests were sequential with ~0.7–1 s
  spacing; no rate limiting was triggered and no host exceeded the ~50-request budget. MileSplit's
  v3/API host answered 403 to a non-browser client, which is a block to record, not to work around.
- **Ancillary observation on Athletic.net (context for the mission's block note).** With a plain
  `curl` and a Chrome `User-Agent` header: `https://www.athletic.net/` → **200** (28,026 bytes) and
  `https://www.athletic.net/team/1/track-and-field-outdoor/2026` → **200** ("New Hope Christian -
  High School Track and Field Outdoor 2026", 9,827 bytes, `og:url` with the canonical
  `/team/<TeamID>/<sport-outdoor>/<year>` shape). With curl's default UA, the same two URLs return
  **403** (5,343 / 5,430 bytes). So the brief's "403 to non-browser clients" finding is a
  User-Agent-header discriminator on these paths, not a blanket Cloudflare challenge. Recorded as an
  observation only — the production pipeline's Chromium-only policy is unchanged by it, and nothing
  in this report depends on Athletic.net access.

### Recommendation

**DirectAthletics — PRIMARY for the Indiana roster/grade/team layers (and ATHLETIC.NET-SEED for the
resulting identities); VALIDATION for the 2025 historical archive.** Treat it as **two hosts with
different jobs**, in this order:

1. **Parent-domain HSR performance lists immediately** (`directathletics.com/lists/track/1428_<id>.html`
   + `1429_<id>.html`). 2 requests per season yield hundreds of Indiana HS athletes **with grade,
   team, event mark, meet name and meet date**, in the shared AthleteID namespace, server-rendered —
   the highest-value-per-request surface found in this assignment (2026: 3,978 from Large + 2,555
   from Small = **6,533 in 2 requests, disjoint**; add **`&year=JR`** for **1,861 Class-of-2027**). Since these are HSR indoor qualifiers they skew to the
   strongest athletes, which is exactly the Class-of-2027 recruiting cohort the mission targets, and
   they carry no paywall and no robots restriction. Two guards: confirm the rendered `title` names the
   right class before merging (a bad list id silently serves the Large list, see D3), and remember the
   **400 relay-team rows at the default limit (775 at `limit=1000`) carry no AthleteID**, so relay-only
   athletes cannot be harvested here.
2. **Parent-domain team universe** — `/leagues/track/123.html` + `/leagues/xc/108.html`, **2 requests
   for 1,816 numeric TeamIDs** (929 T&F + 887 XC) — plus the meet index (`legacy_da/results` with a
   date window) for new-meet discovery including HS duals and middle-school meets the subdomain
   search omits. Read the rendered `Sport:` before trusting a league id: `xc/123` is a college league.
3. **Subdomain roster sweep** (`/leagues/123.html` + 901 team pages) for the complete roster/grade
   layer — the only route found that gives *every* Indiana HS athlete a grade, including
   non-qualifiers, at ≈0.07 requests per Class-of-2027 athlete. Mind the label: the roster serves the
   2025-26 season by default, so Class of 2027 is `JR` there, not `SR`.

That combination serves mission goals 1 (discover athletes outside Athletic.net ranking enumeration),
5 (independently verify Class of 2027) and 6 (verify school identity, via slug, numeric TeamID and the
team's IHSAA sectional/regional league links). It is **not** an outdoor-2026 result source: the
subdomain search shows 56 track meets and 1 XC meet for 2026 against 1,022/128 for 2025, and the
parent-domain index is indoor/HS skewed. A result pipeline keyed to it should expect indoor +
qualifier coverage, not the full outdoor season.

**MileSplit Indiana — DISCOVERY-ONLY (with a genuine VALIDATION asset in `Class of YYYY`).**
Expected marginal coverage: its meet calendar is far broader than TFRRS's for 2026 (≥101 unique
2026 HS outdoor meets and ≥50 2026 HS XC meets in the first page of each filter), so it is the better
source for *meet* discovery and for seeing IHSAA sectional/regional/state rounds plus national meets
(AAU, adidas nationals, Brooks PR) that TFRRS lacks. The 533-team index (with city) and the 7,500-
athlete sitemap window are cheap. But performances are subscription-gated, the enumerable athlete
surface is a non-paginated recency window, and anything deeper requires the `robots.txt`-disallowed
`/api/` or `/rankings`. Recommended use: weekly sitemap diff for new Indiana athletes, team index
for school/city reconciliation, results index for new-meet discovery; treat "Class of YYYY" on a
public profile as independent Class-of-2027 corroboration for athletes that TFRRS or Athletic.net
have already surfaced.

**Neither source is a coach directory** — assign Indiana coach/AD contact acquisition to the
IHSAA/school-directory lane (agents 17/29).
